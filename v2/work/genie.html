Overview

Search inside a company is slow, noisy and scattered.

Genie is an AI-powered plugin that sits beside your browser’s search. It searches across the apps a team already uses, learns what each person cares about, and lets them save, organise and share the links they keep coming back to.

Aravind took care of research; I took care of the user interface and the overall end-to-end UX. Together we went from problem to tested prototype in four weeks.

Problem & solution

Conventional internal search can’t keep up with how teams work.

  • Irrelevant results. People spend extra time filtering through things they didn’t ask for.
  • No personalisation. Search doesn’t learn from behaviour, so it can’t suggest what’s new and useful.
  • Rich media is a blind spot. Images, videos, documents and audio are hard to find.

Our goal: an AI-powered internal search that understands intent, returns accurate results, personalises recommendations and handles every kind of content.

Our solution

An AI plugin that learns from behaviour to personalise results, indexes rich media alongside documents, and lets people save, organise and share what they find — so the right information is always a search away.

Strategy

Who it’s for, and what success looks like.

Business goal
A seamless internal search engine powered by AI.
Target users
Designers, video editors, product managers, sales and marketers.
Platform
Web, as a browser plugin.
  • Success looks like finding the right information, reaching it whenever it’s needed, and sharing and organising links.
  • Everyday tasks: search for information, save links into folders, share them with people.

User interviews

Starting with the people who search every day.

We interviewed people in person and over video calls (Zoom, WhatsApp, Google Meet), and used an online survey to reach more potential users — aiming for 8–10 participants to gather what we needed.

Target audience
Designers, video editors, sales and marketers who want to be more productive and keep their links in order.

Persona

Meet Robert, a graphic designer who can’t afford to search twice.

Robert, graphic designer · Argentina

Gives his best at work and spends his free time learning new design skills and doing a little photography.

“I need search that understands what I’m looking for and saves me time.”

Goals

Find relevant information fast; spend less time searching manually; keep up with trends and competitors.

Frustrations

Irrelevant results; nothing personalised; poor support for documents, videos and presentations.

Needs

Accurate results based on his preferences, in an interface that makes searching effortless.

Empathy map

What Robert says, thinks, does and feels.

Says

  • “I need relevant information quickly.”
  • “Search often shows unrelated results.”
  • “I want an interface that saves me time.”
  • “It should understand my preferences.”

Thinks

  • “I spend too long sifting through results.”
  • “Search should learn from my behaviour.”
  • “It can’t handle different formats.”
  • “I need the latest trends and analysis.”

Does

  • Asks colleagues and outside sources.
  • Struggles to search across formats.
  • Filters results by hand.
  • Raises his frustration with the team.

Feels

  • Annoyed by wasted time.
  • Overwhelmed by the volume of information.
  • Impatient when search is slow.
  • Unproductive when results miss.

Qualitative research

Understanding experiences, not just opinions.

Individual interviews and focus groups with employees, managers and administrators who rely on internal search, to uncover feedback that would shape the AI plugin.

Interview type
Contextual interview
Duration
10 minutes per person
Channel
Google Meet

Questions we asked

  1. How often do you use your organisation’s internal search?
  2. What challenges or frustrations have you had with it?
  3. What features do you wish it had?
  4. How important is personalisation in search?
  5. How easy is the current search to use?
  6. Are you looking for any special features?
  7. What made an experience memorable or effective?
  8. What would you expect from an AI-powered internal search?

Quantitative research

Putting numbers to what we heard.

A survey measured how widespread each need was, to guide what the plugin should do first.

  • 76%need to save important work into different folders
  • 52%want to add people to folders and manage their access
  • 89%need better integrations with the tools they use

Usability testing

Ten people, one prototype.

We tested the high-fidelity prototype with ten potential users, asking them to complete everyday tasks.

What worked

  • Most people completed the tasks.
  • The AI understood their queries and returned relevant results.
  • The interface was well received.

What to improve

  • Add advanced search options for specific queries.
  • Give clear feedback when there are no or few results.

Learnings & next steps

Research changed the product more than any screen did.

Testing showed the plugin worked well for task completion and relevance, and that personalisation and support for different content types mattered a lot to people.

We learned how colour, type and components shape the experience together. Next, we’d research further to flesh out the full web experience.
