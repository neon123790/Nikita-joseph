The Scorecard

Turning a minimal brokerage challenge into a daily scorecard that shows profit, loss, and rewards at a glance.

Role
UX Designer, FYERS
Team

In short
The existing challenge timeline showed how far through the challenge you were, but not how you were doing. I redesigned it as a daily calendar where each trading day’s profit or loss is visible at a glance. I also added badges, a Past Challenges record, and rules shown before a trader starts.
The result
A daily calendar that shows profit, loss, and no-trade days without opening anything. Badges and certificates that make a win feel like an achievement, not only a refund. A Past Challenges record with status and pending payouts.
  • 30trading days on one card
  • 3day states: profit, loss, no trade
  • 5badge levels, Rookie to Wizard
  • ₹3,000minimum profit to qualify for a refund

01Context

Built on the Existing Card

Traders cared about the challenge. The card hadn’t kept up.

In June 2021, FYERS launched My Account, a reporting portal that replaced its back office. The announcement listed the 30 Day Challenge among its features: “30 Day Challenge timeline & results of previous challenges.”

The original view was a single winding line with a date every second day and a user avatar at the end. It showed progress through time, but not performance. You couldn’t see profit or loss on any day, and there was no visible record of wins or losses.

  1. Dates appear every second day
  2. No profit or loss on any day
  3. No outcome, only an avatar at the end

Traders still cared. One trader started with ₹1,800 and said they would share daily P&L in the community. In December 2022, another publicly thanked FYERS for crediting the brokerage on time. The demand was there, but the experience hadn’t kept up.

02Brief

Keep Score Every Day

Make the challenge feel like a streak. A trader should check it every trading day, see each day’s result at a glance, and understand the whole experience without reading the terms.

Where the old card fell short

No data
Progress was visible, but profit and loss were not.
Skipped days
Dates appeared every second day, so the 30-day count was hard to follow.
No outcome
Wins and losses weren’t marked, and past attempts weren’t recorded.

03Constraints

Rules Printed on the Card

The challenge rules changed over time, and the interface had to explain them clearly.

RuleWhat it meant
Minimum profitTraders needed ₹3,000 in profit during the challenge period to qualify for a refund.
Overall P&LAfter the new back office went live, overall P&L across NSE, BSE, and MCX counted toward the result.
Refund capFrom 1 November 2022, a ₹10,000 cap applied to challenges started on or after that date.
API usersClients with API access could join but received certificates instead of refunds, since FYERS offers its API free and wanted the challenge to target discretionary traders.

The interface had to show these rules before a trader started, not leave them in the terms.

04Solution

Redesigning the Card

A calendar laid out as a scorecard

I replaced the winding timeline with a calendar grid. Each trading day has its own card: green for profit, red for loss, grey for no trade. Each card shows the date, weekday, number of trades, and total P&L.

People already read streaks from fitness and language apps. I wanted the challenge to borrow that habit, so a trader could tell where they stood in one glance.

  1. Profit: a green card
  2. Loss: a red card
  3. No trade: a grey card, with its own label
  4. Trades taken that day
  5. Total P&L for that day
  6. Running total for the challenge

Every score can be checked

A green card only reassures if you can check it. Tapping a day opens its trades: symbol, segment, quantity, price, brokerage, and P&L. Traders can verify the number themselves instead of relying on a summary.

  1. The day you tapped, with its total
  2. P&L for every trade
  3. Brokerage for every trade
  4. Totals across the view

Badges earned on the card

The refund alone felt transactional. FYERS had already introduced Badges of Honour in 2020, with five levels from Rookie (one win) to Wizard (five wins in a row). My contribution was the in-account experience around them. When a trader wins, the badge and certificate appear with the result, so the win feels like an accomplishment even when the refund is limited.

Past cards on file

Traders can attempt the challenge more than once, so the calendar alone isn’t enough. The Past Challenges view lists each completed and filled challenge with its status, brokerage, and certificate. It also shows pending payouts, so a trader doesn’t need to raise a ticket to check a refund.

  1. Status: won or failed, in words
  2. Certificate for each win
  3. Badge earned

A bad round, handled kindly

Losing is part of trading. The failure screen gives an encouraging message and a clear choice: try again, or wait. The intent was to bring people back without blame.

05Live Feedback

Voices on the Scorecard

Users praised the new portal. One wrote that it was “beautiful, intuitive and easy to follow,” and another called the UI “beautiful and simple”.

They also reported problems:

Report 01

A challenge went missing

A running challenge didn’t appear in the new tab for some users, though it showed on the home page. One user confirmed it was fixed a day later.

Report 02

Charges left out

The P&L report excluded charges.

Request 03

Show an equity curve

Users asked for an equity curve, similar to what other brokers offer.

Request 04

Finish the QA

One user asked the team to complete QA fully before launching new features.

These points shaped my priorities: consistent data across views, charges included in P&L, and a clearer performance summary.

06Edge Cases

Scores That Don’t Add Up

Payout delays, reconciliation issues, and mid-month starts fell outside the standard flow and went to customer care. The interface showed status but didn’t resolve back-end exceptions. The new back office was meant to speed up certificates and refunds, and the team had flagged earlier processing delays as a problem.

07Accessibility

Reading the Card Without Colour

The calendar relies heavily on red and green, so I designed around colour rather than through it.

Signals
Colour isn’t the only signal. Profit carries a plus sign and loss a minus sign. Each status has a text label, and “No Trade” has its own label.
Plain text
Content works without colour. Dates, weekdays, trade counts, and P&L values are plain text, not part of a graphic.

What I’d audit or improve now

  • Check contrast on small grey labels over tinted backgrounds.
  • Make each card keyboard-focusable, with a visible focus state.
  • Add screen-reader labels that announce the full day in one string, for example: “1 November, profit of ten thousand rupees, four trades.”
  • Test the palette against common forms of colour blindness, and consider patterns or icons on the cards.

08Outcome

How the Scorecard Landed

Traders responded in the community. They called the new portal “beautiful, intuitive and easy to follow,” and they also reported specific problems with data and QA, a sign they were reading it closely.

I can’t share participation or win figures from this project. If I ran it again, I’d judge success by how many participants check the calendar each trading day, how many reach day 30, how many start another challenge, and whether refund-status tickets go down.

From losing a few to winning some, when I look back, it reminds of my trading journey of ups and downs.

FYERS community member, January 2023

09Reflect

What the Scorecard Taught Me

Next rounds

  • Show rules before starting. Minimum profit, excluded segments, and the API difference should be visible up front.
  • Add a payout tracker. Past Challenges could show states such as “under review,” “processed,” and “expected by [date].”
  • Add a risk reminder. A profit-linked reward can push traders into riskier trades, so the copy should discourage that and avoid framing the challenge as easy money.
  • Fix data consistency. Every date, weekday, and P&L figure should come from one source, with charges included.
  • Validate with users. Run a short usability test on the calendar and colour system before the next release.

This is a retrospective review. Screens are recreated with sample data, and account names, IDs, and certificate signatures are removed.

The old card showed how far you’d come. The Scorecard shows how you’re doing, every day.
