The Scorecard

Turning a minimal brokerage challenge into a daily scorecard that shows profit, loss, and rewards at a glance.

In short
The existing challenge timeline showed how far a trader had progressed, but not how they were doing. I redesigned it as a daily calendar, where each trading day’s profit or loss is visible at a glance. I also added badges, a Past Challenges record, and rules shown before a trader starts.
The result
A daily calendar that shows profit, loss, and no-trade days without opening anything. Badges and certificates that make a win feel like an achievement, not only a discount. A Past Challenges record with status and pending payouts.

The outcome

  • 60ktraders joined the 30-day challenge
  • 30trading days on one card
  • 3day states: profit, loss, no trade
  • ₹3,000minimum net profit to qualify
  • ₹10,000maximum brokerage discount per challenge

01Context

Built on the Existing Card

Traders cared about the challenge, but the card hadn’t kept up.

In June 2021, FYERS launched My Account, a reporting portal, and listed the 30 Day Challenge among its features.

The original view was a single winding line with a date every second day and an avatar at the end. It showed progress through time, but not performance. Traders couldn’t see profit or loss on any day, and there was no visible record of past attempts.

  1. Dates appear every second day
  2. No profit or loss on any day
  3. No outcome, only an avatar at the end

Traders were still engaged. One started with ₹1,800 and planned to share daily P&L in the community. Another publicly thanked FYERS for processing a brokerage discount on time.

02Brief

Keep Score Every Day

Make the challenge feel like a streak. A trader should check it every trading day, see each day’s result at a glance, and understand the experience without reading the terms.

Where the old card fell short

Missing data
Progress was visible, but profit and loss were not.
Skipped days
Dates appeared every second day, so the 30-day count was hard to follow.
No outcome
Wins and losses weren’t marked, and past attempts weren’t recorded.

Design principles

  1. Show, don’t summariseEvery day’s result is visible without a tap.
  2. Make it checkableAny number should be traceable to its trades.
  3. Explain before committingRules appear before a trader starts.
  4. Celebrate and recoverWins earn recognition, and losses lead to a clear next step.

03Constraints

Rules on the Card

The challenge rules changed over time, so the interface had to explain them clearly.

RuleWhat it meant for the design
Minimum profitTraders needed at least ₹3,000 in net profit during the challenge period to qualify.
Overall P&LAfter the new back office went live, overall P&L across NSE, BSE, and MCX counted toward the result.
Brokerage discount capFYERS offered a brokerage discount of up to ₹10,000 per challenge.
API usersAPI users could join and received certificates instead of brokerage discounts. FYERS said the challenge was meant to reward discretionary discipline.

These rules were shown before a trader started, not left in the terms.

04Solution

Redesigning the Card

A calendar laid out as a scorecard

I replaced the winding timeline with a calendar grid. Each trading day has its own card: green for profit, red for loss, and grey for no trade. Each card shows the date, weekday, number of trades, and total P&L.

People already read streaks in fitness and language apps. I wanted the challenge to use that habit, so a trader could see where they stood in one glance.

  1. Profit: a green card
  2. Loss: a red card
  3. No trade: a grey card, with its own label
  4. Trades taken that day
  5. Total P&L for that day
  6. Running total for the challenge

Every score can be checked

A green card only reassures if you can check it. Tapping a day opens its trades: symbol, segment, quantity, price, brokerage, and P&L. Traders can verify the numbers themselves instead of relying on a summary.

  1. The day you tapped, with its total
  2. P&L for every trade
  3. Brokerage for every trade
  4. Totals across the view

Badges earned on the card

FYERS had introduced Badges of Honour in 2020, and later added more for traders who won several challenges in a row. My contribution was the in-account experience around them. When a trader wins, the badge and certificate appear with the result, so the win feels like an accomplishment even when the discount is limited.

Past cards on file

Traders could attempt the challenge more than once, so the calendar alone wasn’t enough. The Past Challenges view lists each attempt with its status, brokerage discount, and certificate. Pending payouts are shown too, so a trader doesn’t need to raise a ticket to check.

  1. Status: won or failed, in words
  2. Certificate for each win
  3. Badge earned

A bad round, handled kindly

Losing is part of trading. The failure screen gives an encouraging message and a clear choice: try again, or come back later. The aim was to bring traders back without blame.

05Live Feedback

What Traders Said

Users praised the new portal. One wrote that it was “beautiful, intuitive and easy to follow,” and another called the UI “beautiful and simple”.

Discipline
Traders said the challenge helped them trade more carefully. One credited it with building trading psychology and stopping overtrading. Another described its “psychological reinforcement”.
Continuity
A trader suggested keeping a plain certificate without monetary rewards, which shows the goal itself mattered to them.

Requests that shaped next steps

Request 01

Charges in P&L

Users wanted the report to include charges.

Request 02

Consistent data

A running challenge appeared on the home page but not in the new tab. One user confirmed it was fixed the next day.

Request 03

Equity curve

Users asked for a performance view similar to other brokers.

06Edge Cases

Handled Behind the Scenes

Payout timing, reconciliation, and mid-month starts went through customer care. The interface showed each status clearly, while the back office handled exceptions.

07Accessibility

Colour Is Never the Only Signal

The calendar uses red and green, so every state has a second cue.

Text with colour
Profit carries a plus sign and loss a minus sign. Each status has a written label, including “No Trade.”
Plain text for data
Dates, weekdays, trade counts, and P&L are readable text, not part of a graphic.

Next audit steps

  • Check contrast on small grey labels over tinted backgrounds.
  • Add keyboard focus states to each day card.
  • Write screen-reader labels that read a full day in one line, such as “1 November, profit of ten thousand rupees, four trades.”
  • Test with colour-blind users, and add patterns or icons where needed.

08Outcome

A Short Run

The scorecard shipped in late 2022. In January 2023, FYERS discontinued the challenge with immediate effect. Under SEBI circulars, the challenge had been interpreted as a game or competition. FYERS sought clarifications and argued that it was neither, since it offered brokerage discounts and no prizes.

The product ended for regulatory reasons, not because traders stopped valuing it.

  • 60ktraders joined the 30-day challenge

Within its short run, the scorecard let around 60k traders check each day’s result and verify their trades.

From losing a few to winning some, when I look back, it reminds of my trading journey of ups and downs.

FYERS community member, January 2023

09Reflect

What I Learned

Bring in compliance early
In regulated finance, legal review should run alongside design. Interface polish can’t fix a format that is later questioned.
Show all costs
P&L should include charges from day one. Transparency builds more trust than a clean number.
Frame it around discipline
The challenge was about reducing emotional trading, so the interface should say “record” rather than “streak.”
Test early, with one source of truth
A short usability test on the calendar and colour system would have caught accessibility gaps. Dates, weekdays, and P&L should come from one backend source, so the home page and challenge tab always match.

Next steps

  • Show minimum profit, excluded segments, and the API difference before a trader starts.
  • Add a payout tracker: “under review,” “processed,” and “expected by [date].”
  • Add a risk reminder, since profit-linked rewards can encourage riskier trading.
  • Run usability testing with traders before the next release.

This is a retrospective review. Screens are recreated with sample data, and account names, IDs, and certificate signatures are removed.

The old card showed how far you’d come. The Scorecard showed how you were doing, every day.
