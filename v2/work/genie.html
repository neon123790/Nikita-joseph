Overview

Search inside a company is slow, noisy and scattered.

Genie is a plugin that sits beside your browser’s search. It searches across the apps a team already uses, learns what each person cares about, and lets them save and share the links they keep coming back to.

Aravind led research; I took care of the user interface and the overall end-to-end UX. Together we went from problem to tested prototype in four weeks.

The problem

Conventional internal search can’t keep up with how teams work.

  • Irrelevant results. People spend extra time filtering through things they didn’t ask for.
  • No personalisation. Search doesn’t learn from behaviour, so it can’t suggest what’s new and useful.
  • Rich media is a blind spot. Images, videos, documents and audio are hard to index and find.

Our goal: a seamless internal search engine, powered by AI, that understands intent, returns accurate results and handles every kind of content.

Strategy

Who it’s for, and what success looks like.

Business goal

A seamless internal search engine powered by AI.

Target users

Designers, video editors, product managers, sales and marketers.

Platform

Web, as a browser plugin.

Success means people can: find the right information and reach it whenever they need it; save links into folders; organise them; and share them with the right people.

Research

Talking to people first, then checking with numbers.

Interviews

Contextual interviews over Google Meet, about 10 minutes each, with employees, managers and administrators who rely on internal search. We asked things like:

  • How often do you use your organisation’s internal search?
  • What frustrates you about it? Can you give an example?
  • What do you wish it could do that it can’t today?
  • How important is personalisation in search?
  • What would you expect from an internal search engine powered by AI?

Survey

  • 76%need to save important work into different folders
  • 52%want to add people to folders and manage their access
  • 89%need better integrations with the tools they use

Persona

Meet Robert, a graphic designer who can’t afford to search twice.

Bio. A graphic designer who gives his best at work and spends his free time learning new design skills and doing a little photography.

Goals. Find relevant information quickly; spend less time on manual searching so he can focus on strategy; keep up with trends, reports and competitors.

Frustrations. Internal search returns irrelevant results; nothing is personalised; and it struggles with documents, videos and presentations.

Needs. Accurate, relevant results based on his preferences, in an interface that makes searching effortless.

“Finding the right information quickly is crucial for my work. I need search that understands what I’m looking for and saves me time.”

Empathy map

What Robert says, thinks, does and feels.

Says

“The current search often shows unrelated results and wastes my time.”

Thinks

“I wish the search could learn from my behaviour and deliver tailored results.”

Does

Asks colleagues instead of searching, and manually filters through results.

Feels

Unproductive when results are slow, cumbersome or not relevant enough.

Design

A side panel that sits next to search, not in its way.

Wireframes explored a panel that opens beside the browser’s search page, with a bottom bar for the main areas:

  • Search: one box across connected apps, with recent searches
  • Folders and bookmarks: saved links in a grid or list
  • Sharing: add people to a folder and choose their access
  • Notifications and settings, including the integrations people asked for

Visual design. A calm dark interface with a green accent, set in Poppins for clear, friendly reading at small sizes.

Testing

Ten people, one prototype.

We tested the prototype with ten potential users.

What worked

Most people completed the tasks. They felt the AI understood their queries and returned relevant results, and they liked the interface.

What to improve

Add advanced search options for more specific queries, and give clear feedback when there are no or few results.

What we learned

Research changed the product more than any screen did.

Testing showed the plugin worked well for task completion and relevance, and that personalisation and support for different content types really mattered to people.

We learned how much colour, type and components shape the experience together. Next, we’d research further to flesh out the web experience and its structure.
